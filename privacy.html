Privacy policy

Last updated 19 November 2026. This policy covers the Backchannel app for Android (package uk.jj.backchannel) and this website. Backchannel is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • Backchannel has no accounts and no servers. The developer receives nothing from your phone and cannot see, read, open or recover your messages or contacts.
  • Messages go directly from your phone to your contact's phone over the Tor network, encrypted so that only that phone can open them.
  • There is no advertising and no analytics in the app.
  • Everything Backchannel keeps is on your phone, encrypted, in storage that other apps cannot read. It is deleted when you uninstall the app.

What stays on your phone

Backchannel keeps the following in its private storage, in an encrypted database:

  • Your messages, photos and voice notes, sent and received.
  • Your contacts: each one's address on Tor, their public identity key, the name they chose or the one you gave them, and, if you linked one, a small copy of their address-book picture.
  • This phone's own keys and its address on Tor, and the name you chose for your contacts to see, if any.

Outside the database it keeps the database key, stored only in locked form (locked with your PIN or passphrase and with the phone's security hardware), a count of wrong unlock attempts, and a few settings such as the colour theme. Your PIN, passphrase and duress PIN are never stored.

None of this is included in Android's backup or in a transfer to a new phone. The only copy that leaves the app is a backup file, and only when you make one and choose where it goes.

What is sent, and to whom

  • To a contact: the messages, photos and voice notes you send them, the name you chose (if any), and changes you make to a chat's disappearing-message timer. All of it is encrypted end to end. Their phone also learns your phone's address on Tor, which is how it reaches you. It does not learn your internet address, your location or your phone number.
  • To the Tor network: the encrypted traffic itself. The computers that relay it cannot read it and cannot tell who is talking to whom.
  • To the developer: nothing.

Photos are re-encoded before sending, which removes location, camera details and other information stored inside the picture.

Services the app contacts

  • The Tor network. Tor is run by volunteers around the world and is not operated by the developer. Its relays carry Backchannel's encrypted traffic. See the Tor Project for how it works.
  • GitHub, for the update check. Once a day, and when you tap Check now, the app asks GitHub's public service for the number of the newest published version. The request is made through Tor, so GitHub sees a Tor address and not yours, and it contains nothing about you or your phone. You can turn the daily check off in Settings.

The app contacts nothing else.

Other people

The messages you receive, and the contacts you keep, are about other people. They stay on your phone and are yours to manage. A contact can keep what you send them, and can photograph their screen, whatever timer is set. You are responsible for what you send and for how you use what you receive.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • A chat's disappearing-message timer deletes its messages from both phones after the time you set.
  • Deleting a contact removes them, every message, photo and voice note with them, and the keys you share, from your phone.
  • Typing your duress PIN at the lock screen destroys the keys and then everything the app holds.
  • Clearing the app's storage or uninstalling Backchannel deletes everything it holds.
  • A backup file you have made is an ordinary file wherever you saved it. Delete it there when you no longer want it.
  • Deleting something from your phone does not delete the copy on your contact's phone.
  • Nothing is held by the developer, so there is nothing further to delete.

Your rights

UK and EU data protection law gives you rights over personal data that an organisation holds about you, including the right to see it, correct it and have it deleted.

The developer of Backchannel holds no personal data about you: there is no account, no server and no log of who uses the app. What Backchannel stores is on your own phone, where you control it directly using the steps above. If you need help, use the contact link below. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Security

Messages are encrypted end to end with the Signal Protocol. Stored data is encrypted with SQLCipher (AES-256), under a key locked with your PIN or passphrase using Argon2id and again with a key held in the phone's security hardware. Backup files are encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a key made from the backup passphrase.

No system is perfectly secure. Backchannel is at an early version and has not had an independent security review. It cannot protect you from spy software on your own phone, from someone holding your unlocked phone, or from the person you are talking to. The limits are set out on the home page.

This website

This website sets no cookies and has no analytics, advertising or tracking. It is hosted by GitHub Pages, and the app file is downloaded from GitHub. Like any web host, GitHub receives your internet address and browser details when you visit or download. See the GitHub privacy statement.

Children

Backchannel is not aimed at children and collects no information from anyone.

Changes

If the app changes in a way that affects this policy, the policy will be updated and the date at the top changed.

Contact
